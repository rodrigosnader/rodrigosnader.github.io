# Gamifying cooperation — Intent & Structure

## Core insight

Political extremism is rational under current incentives. Defection is rewarded (base mobilization, media attention, tribal solidarity). Cooperation is punished (looks like weakness, gives credit to the other side). You don't need better voters or better candidates to fix this. You need to flip the reward function. Specifically: tie campaign funding to cross-party bill support, and the dominant strategy reverses. Cooperation suddenly becomes the path to resources, not the path to political death.

## The arc

1. **The current equilibrium.** Defection is the dominant strategy. Blocking everything mobilizes the base and gets media coverage. Cooperation looks weak.
2. **Why moral appeals fail.** Telling politicians to "work across the aisle" doesn't change incentives. You're asking them to accept lower payoffs for the same effort.
3. **The structural fix.** Tie funding to cross-party support. The math flips.
4. **How to measure cooperation without gaming.** Thresholds, independent tracking, weighted outcomes. Symbolic votes don't count the same as structural reforms. Obstruction without an alternative gets penalized.
5. **The shadow of the future.** Short-term defection looks good, but sustained cooperation compounds under stable funding rules.
6. **Why this is hard but not impossible.** Engineering-hard, not physics-hard.
7. **What could go wrong.** Fake bipartisanship. Measuring the wrong thing. One party dropping out entirely. Base still rewarding purity.
