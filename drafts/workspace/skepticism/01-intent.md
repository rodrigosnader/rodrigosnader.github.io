# Why truth is narrow — Intent & Structure

## Core insight

False statements outnumber true ones by an unimaginable margin. For every claim that accurately describes reality, there are infinite ways to distort, misrepresent, and invent falsehoods. If you don't actively filter information, you'll believe something false more often than something true — not because you're stupid, but because the space of ways to be wrong is so much larger than the space of ways to be right. Skepticism isn't cynicism. It's the only rational response to the asymmetry.

## The arc

1. **The setup.** Are there more possible true statements or false ones?
2. **The answer that should worry you.** Infinitely more false ones.
3. **What this means for your beliefs.** Without active filtering, you'll believe false things more often.
4. **The lock metaphor.** A lock with 1,000 combinations; only one opens the door. Truth is narrow.
5. **The "open mind" trap.** An open mind without skepticism is just an open door for nonsense.
6. **The wrong default question.** Most people ask "could this be true?" They should ask "could this be false?"
7. **Why this is urgent.** Health, money, history — the cost of accepting by default has never been higher.
8. **The deeper cost.** If you don't filter, someone else will — and you won't know when they lied.

## Target reader

Someone who considers themselves "open-minded" and quietly suspects skepticism is for cynics. Believes they're good at separating fact from fiction. The goal is to make them feel the asymmetry viscerally — to realize their confidence in their own judgment is a blind spot.

## Animation ideas

- **Hero: The sieve.** A grid of dots representing the space of all possible statements. A tiny region is "true"; the rest is false. A slider controls "skepticism tightness." As the slider tightens, the grid dims except for the surviving dots. The point: tightening the filter doesn't reduce truth — it increases the ratio of true claims that get through.
- **Optional: The lock.** 1,000 slots visualized; only one is correct. User toggles "verification level" — watches how many wrong combinations they'd accept at each level.

Pick the sieve; it's stronger. The post is happy with one clean viz.

First-person plural ("we", "our") — inclusive, not accusatory. Alert without being alarmist.
